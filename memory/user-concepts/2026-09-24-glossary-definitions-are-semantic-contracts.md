# User Concept — Glossary definitions are semantic contracts

Date: 2026-09-24
Status: **ACTIVE / CANONICAL TERMINOLOGY POLICY**

## USER-STATED

Definitions do not have to be simplified.

They may be as large as necessary to make sure understanding is not lost.

## SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE

Canonical Floware glossary entries should be treated as **semantic contracts**, not merely dictionary blurbs.

A full definition should preserve enough information to recover:
- what the concept is;
- what it contains;
- what it does;
- what it does not do;
- its boundaries;
- its invariants;
- its relationships to neighboring concepts;
- common confusions/failure modes;
- how the concept behaves when composed with the rest of Floware.

Short forms may still exist for recall, navigation, and conversation, but they are non-authoritative mnemonics.

Core rule:

> **Compress for recall, not for authority. Preserve the full meaning in the canonical definition.**

If a concise explanation omits something material, the full canonical glossary entry controls.

## IMMEDIATE APPLICATION

The Floware, Pipe, Pipeline, and Plumbing entries were expanded so they preserve:
- the older state/evidence/goalpost/learning/software-boundary understanding of Floware;
- the newer Flow/Pipe/Pipeline/Plumbing ontology;
- Pipe contract and integrity semantics;
- Pipeline composition and failure semantics;
- Plumbing topology/state/provenance/authority implications.

This is a terminology-governance rule and should apply to future glossary entries as well.
