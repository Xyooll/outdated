# BOT-008 — Garlic (Allium sativum)

**Identity / provenance:** Central Asia origin; global food and medicine  
**Part and preparation scope:** bulb; chopping generates allicin, aging creates a different profile

## Known / reported priority compounds

- **Alliin** — stable precursor converted by alliinase upon crushing.
- **Allicin** — reactive sulfur compound with laboratory antimicrobial actions, unstable.
- **Ajoene** — sulfur-derived molecule formed from allicin, platelet effects studied experimentally.
- **Diallyl disulfide** — volatile sulfur compound with preclinical signaling effects.
- **Diallyl trisulfide** — sulfur compound studied for vascular signaling.
- **S-Allyl cysteine** — more prominent in aged garlic extracts and studied in human formulations.

## Four-domain effects (not all clinically proven)

- **B – bioenergetic/metabolic:** Some formulated supplements show modest blood-pressure or lipid marker changes.
- **P – physiological:** Vascular effects studied clinically; topical antimicrobial lab results do not justify applying garlic to wounds.
- **C – cognitive/neurocognitive:** No clear clinical memory benefit.
- **A – affective/psychophysiological:** No direct mood efficacy established.

## Evidence level and limits

Human clinical studies on selected garlic preparations; chemistry dramatically preparation-dependent.

## Global / traditional context

Global culinary/folk medicine, including South and East Asian systems.

## Combination map / identity links

BOT-008 ↔ BOT-019 blood-pressure pathways might add; not safe to assume clinically.

## Safety and unknowns

Bleeding interaction concerns and surgery precautions; raw topical garlic can severely burn skin.
