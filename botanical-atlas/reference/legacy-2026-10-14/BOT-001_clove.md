# BOT-001 — Clove (Syzygium aromaticum)

**Identity / provenance:** Maluku Islands, Indonesia; used throughout Asia, Africa and elsewhere  
**Part and preparation scope:** dried flower buds; oil is not equivalent to whole buds

## Known / reported priority compounds

- **Eugenol** — dominant volatile; affects pain signaling and microbial membranes in laboratory settings.
- **Eugenyl acetate** — aromatic ester and potential eugenol precursor; human role poorly isolated.
- **β-caryophyllene** — terpene with CB2 receptor activity shown experimentally.
- **α-humulene** — sesquiterpene with preclinical anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Gallic acid** — phenolic antioxidant in laboratory models.
- **Quercetin** — flavonoid modulating inflammatory signaling preclinically.
- **Kaempferol** — flavonoid studied for cell-signaling effects.
- **Ellagic acid** — tannin-related polyphenol.
- **Caffeic acid** — common plant phenolic.

## Four-domain effects (not all clinically proven)

- **B – bioenergetic/metabolic:** No demonstrated meaningful energy increase from culinary use.
- **P – physiological:** Local dental numbing has a plausible eugenol mechanism; lab antibacterial/antifungal findings do not prove infection treatment.
- **C – cognitive/neurocognitive:** No reliable human memory improvement established.
- **A – affective/psychophysiological:** Aromatic sensory effects are possible; antidepressant claims are unproved.

## Evidence level and limits

Composition well characterized; topical/dental use more established than systemic clinical claims.

## Global / traditional context

Indonesian spice tradition; Dingxiang (丁香) in Chinese materia medica for cold-type digestive symptoms.

## Combination map / identity links

BOT-001 ↔ BOT-012 (eugenol shared); BOT-001 ↔ BOT-005 (eugenol shared, especially cinnamon leaf); BOT-001 ↔ BOT-002 (overlapping membrane-active aromatic phenols; synergy unproven).

## Safety and unknowns

Concentrated clove oil can burn tissue; swallowing amounts can cause severe liver injury, especially in children; allergy possible.
