# BOT-019 — Passionflower (Passiflora incarnata)

**Identity / provenance:** Americas; Native American knowledge and later European herbal medicine  
**Part and preparation scope:** aerial parts; species identification matters

## Known / reported priority compounds

- **Vitexin** — C-glycosyl flavone.
- **Isovitexin** — position-isomer, characteristic extract marker.
- **Orientin** — C-glycosyl luteolin.
- **Isoorientin** — related isomer.
- **Schaftoside** — C-glycosyl flavonoid.
- **Apigenin** — possible nervous-system receptor interactions in models.
- **Chrysin** — often mentioned but low concentration/bioavailability.
- **Harmine/harmaline** — historical TRACE reports often absent from commercial P. incarnata.

## Four-domain effects (not all clinically proven)

- **B – bioenergetic/metabolic:** No reliable energy effect.
- **P – physiological:** Some calming/sleep claims but not proven for chronic insomnia.
- **C – cognitive/neurocognitive:** Sedation may impair attention rather than improve it.
- **A – affective/psychophysiological:** Small short-term trials suggest anxiety changes but evidence limited.

## Evidence level and limits

Chemical identity well studied; human outcomes remain preliminary.

## Global / traditional context

Traditional American plant later incorporated into European herbal practice.

## Combination map / identity links

BOT-019 ↔ BOT-018 potential additive sedation; caution, evidence not an endorsement.

## Safety and unknowns

Potential sedation/drug interaction; do not infer potent MAOI effects from trace disputed alkaloid reports.
