# BOT-020 — Licorice (Glycyrrhiza glabra)

**Identity / provenance:** Mediterranean, West Asia; Unani, Ayurveda and broader medicine  
**Part and preparation scope:** root; deglycyrrhizinated products have different risk profiles

## Known / reported priority compounds

- **Glycyrrhizin** — saponin converted to glycyrrhetinic acid by gut bacteria.
- **Glycyrrhetinic acid** — inhibits 11β-HSD2, causing cortisol to mimic aldosterone in kidneys.
- **Liquiritin** — flavonoid glycoside.
- **Liquiritigenin** — flavanone with experimental receptor effects.
- **Glabridin** — isoflavan with preclinical signaling.
- **Isoliquiritigenin** — chalcone with experimental anti-inflammatory effects.

## Four-domain effects (not all clinically proven)

- **B – bioenergetic/metabolic:** Electrolyte disturbances can cause weakness, not vitality.
- **P – physiological:** Can raise blood pressure and lower potassium; strongest clinically relevant mechanism here.
- **C – cognitive/neurocognitive:** No reliable therapeutic cognition effect.
- **A – affective/psychophysiological:** Traditional soothing use does not establish anxiolysis.

## Evidence level and limits

Well-established adverse physiology; many positive mechanisms are only preclinical.

## Global / traditional context

Gan cao in Chinese medicine and mulethi in South Asian usage; common formula ingredient traditionally.

## Combination map / identity links

BOT-020 ↔ BOT-008 blood-pressure effects may oppose or complicate each other—do not mix to balance them.

## Safety and unknowns

Serious hypertension, edema, low potassium, arrhythmia; risks with diuretics, digoxin, heart/kidney disease and pregnancy.
