# FUN-032 — Chaga (Inonotus obliquus)

**Identity / provenance:** Boreal forests of Eurasia and North America; Siberian/northern folk traditions  
**Part and preparation scope:** sterile conk/sclerotial mass on birch; not a normal edible mushroom fruiting body

## Known / reported priority compounds

- **Inotodiol** — lanostane triterpenoid studied in laboratory inflammatory/tumor pathways.
- **Trametenolic acid** — lanostane triterpenoid.
- **Betulin** — birch-derived triterpene depending on substrate.
- **Betulinic acid** — oxidized derivative studied preclinically.
- **Melanin-like polymers** — antioxidant-assay-active pigments.
- **Polysaccharides** — varied high-molecular-weight fractions.
- **Oxalates** — potentially harmful compounds important to identity, not health-promoting actives.

## Four-domain effects (not all clinically proven)

- **B – bioenergetic/metabolic:** No validated energy improvement.
- **P – physiological:** Human disease-treatment evidence weak; kidney harm has been reported with high/long-term use.
- **C – cognitive/neurocognitive:** Brain-benefit claims are unproven.
- **A – affective/psychophysiological:** No established mood effect.

## Evidence level and limits

Mostly cell/animal laboratory work and northern traditional use; toxicity case reports are human evidence.

## Global / traditional context

Chaga used in Siberian/Russian and northern regional traditions.

## Combination map / identity links

FUN-032 ↔ FUN-026 shared triterpene-class research; do NOT equate their safety or combine as a kidney-safe tonic.

## Safety and unknowns

High oxalate exposure has caused oxalate kidney injury, including severe renal failure; especially concerning with kidney disease/stones.
