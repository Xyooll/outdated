# BOT-005 — Ceylon cinnamon (Cinnamomum verum)

**Identity / provenance:** Sri Lanka and South Asia; traded globally  
**Part and preparation scope:** inner bark; leaf oil has different chemistry from bark oil

## Known / reported priority compounds

- **trans-Cinnamaldehyde** — primary bark aroma, reacts with proteins and affects microbial/ metabolic signaling in labs.
- **Eugenol** — much richer in leaf oil, see BOT-001.
- **Procyanidins** — polyphenol oligomers studied for metabolic effects.
- **Cinnamic acid** — aromatic acid.
- **Cinnamyl acetate** — aroma ester.
- **Coumarin** — far lower in Ceylon than most cassia species, a critical identity marker.

## Four-domain effects (not all clinically proven)

- **B – bioenergetic/metabolic:** Blood-glucose research across cinnamon species is inconsistent; do not transfer findings automatically.
- **P – physiological:** Antimicrobial effects often test concentrated extracts, not food portions.
- **C – cognitive/neurocognitive:** No convincing cognitive improvement.
- **A – affective/psychophysiological:** Aroma has sensory associations, not clinical mood treatment.

## Evidence level and limits

Human studies are heterogeneous and often mix cinnamon species.

## Global / traditional context

Sri Lankan culinary and South/West Asian medicinal traditions.

## Combination map / identity links

BOT-005 ↔ BOT-001 via eugenol, principally cinnamon LEAF oil; BOT-005 ↔ BOT-023 possible shared glycemic pathway, not demonstrated synergy.

## Safety and unknowns

Cassia substitutions can have much higher coumarin and liver risk; oil can irritate.
