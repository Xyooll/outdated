# BOT-012 — Holy basil / tulsi (Ocimum tenuiflorum (syn. O. sanctum))

**Identity / provenance:** Indian subcontinent; Ayurveda and Hindu cultural traditions  
**Part and preparation scope:** leaves, flowering tops; essential oil versus leaf extract

## Known / reported priority compounds

- **Eugenol** — aroma phenol also in clove.
- **Rosmarinic acid** — phenolic potentially influencing inflammatory pathways.
- **Ursolic acid** — triterpene with preclinical metabolic effects.
- **Apigenin** — flavone.
- **Luteolin** — flavone.
- **β-Caryophyllene** — aromatic terpene acting on CB2 in experimental systems.

## Four-domain effects (not all clinically proven)

- **B – bioenergetic/metabolic:** Some small stress/metabolic trials; no dependable energy transformation.
- **P – physiological:** Experimental inflammatory and immune signals; clinical proof limited.
- **C – cognitive/neurocognitive:** No robust human cognition treatment.
- **A – affective/psychophysiological:** Small trials suggest subjective stress changes, formulations heterogeneous.

## Evidence level and limits

Traditional observations plus preliminary human and mechanistic data.

## Global / traditional context

Tulsi has sacred and household significance in India; do not reduce cultural role to chemistry alone.

## Combination map / identity links

BOT-012 ↔ BOT-001 eugenol; BOT-012 ↔ BOT-003 rosmarinic/ursolic acid.

## Safety and unknowns

Concentrated extracts may affect glucose/clotting and interact with medication; pregnancy caution.
