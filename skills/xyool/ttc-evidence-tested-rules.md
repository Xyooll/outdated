# Time to Catalyst — Evidence-Tested Rules

This file records TTC rules promoted, rejected, or frozen from sequential historical holdouts. It supplements `time-to-catalyst.md`. Failed replays are preserved and are never rewritten as wins.

## Promoted — New Capital vs Earned Capital Split

Status: **PROMOTED after three fresh holdouts**.

Evidence after the hypothesis was generated:

1. Container-shipping scarcity holdout: split policy improved terminal capital versus the prior allocation-and-hold process.
2. Steel-capacity holdout: split policy improved terminal capital versus the prior allocation-and-hold process.
3. Metallurgical/thermal coal scarcity holdout: split policy improved terminal capital versus the prior allocation-and-hold process.

Pooled fresh-holdout result:

```text
Prior initial allocation + hold: $1,404,883
TTC-equivalent split behavior:   $1,455,118
Improvement:                     +$50,235 / +3.58%
Fresh cohort wins:               3 / 3
```

### Rule

**NEW CAPITAL** — cash, deposits, dividends, harvested proceeds, or genuinely unallocated capital:
- allocate to the best current qualified TTC opportunity;
- allow aggressive preference for a clearly superior return-velocity case.

**EARNED CAPITAL** — capital already inside an earned P4+/C3+ or otherwise qualified position with intact causal thesis:
- do not remove merely because another opportunity improved;
- require a higher removal/replacement hurdle.

### Earned-capital removal hurdle

Material reduction requires one or more of:
1. confirmation or quality downgrade;
2. bottleneck/control deterioration;
3. remaining-opportunity compression;
4. catalyst failure, staleness, or material delay;
5. destructive shareholder friction;
6. a competing qualified opportunity clears a large TTC replacement hurdle;
7. recognition materially outruns economics and Quant confirms a real risk/trend break.

High volatility alone may cap additions but does not force a core reduction.

## Rejected — Aggressive continuous rotation among healthy qualified winners

Status: **REJECT / NOT A TTC AUTHORITY**.

A seven-cohort winner-heavy stress test shows why TTC must not become a universal ranking-and-reshuffling engine.

Existing event/split behavior versus equal-weight buy-and-hold:

```text
Container shipping:              WIN
Steel capacity:                  LOSS
Coal scarcity:                   LOSS
Software control points:         LOSS
LTL / freight networks:          LOSS
Semiconductor subsystem tools:   small WIN
U.S. gas / LNG:                  small WIN
```

Pooled result:

```text
Equal-weight total:      $2,228,497
Event/split total:       $2,338,137
Pooled difference:       +$109,640 / +4.92%
Cohort win rate:         3 / 7
```

The pooled excess is materially influenced by the container-shipping cohort. The low cohort win rate means the data do **not** establish a stable generic stock-to-stock rotation edge among simultaneously healthy winners.

Permanent interpretation:

> **A relative TTC rank change among qualified, intact holdings is not sufficient by itself to force capital movement.**

This strengthens the existing rule to avoid forced reallocation among simultaneously strengthening opportunities.

## Supported — Payoff Persistence as qualitative economic-state information

Status: **DIAGNOSTIC / CONCEPTUAL SUPPORT; NO NUMERIC MULTIPLIER**.

A 2026-09-15 diagnostic tested whether a catalyst that activates recurring/run-rate economics should receive different TTC treatment from a one-off award or milestone.

### Rejected simple hypothesis

Reject:

> **Recurring / run-rate activation automatically produces more persistent stock-price appreciation than a one-off order.**

The diagnostic cases contradicted that claim. A recurring activation can be economically persistent while price subsequently falls because recognition was already high or financing, valuation, Quality, shareholder capture or later execution evidence dominates. A discrete production order can also produce strong medium-window repricing.

### Supported distinction

The useful rule is narrower:

> **When a timer realizes, TTC should distinguish whether the economic payoff ends with the event or activates an ongoing economic stream.**

This is a post-timer economic-state field, not a new timer and not a direct trading signal.

Use qualitative states:
- ONE-OFF REALIZATION;
- REPEATABLE DISCRETE;
- RUN-RATE ACTIVATION;
- STACKED / OVERLAPPING RUN-RATE;
- DECAYING / TERMINABLE RUN-RATE.

The post-realization state may update:
- remaining opportunity;
- catalyst magnitude;
- return velocity;
- recognition-gap interpretation;
- Quality/shareholder-capture evidence where relevant;
- the next unresolved timer.

### Economic Persistence ≠ Price-Recognition Persistence

Economic Persistence asks whether ongoing company economics survive after the catalyst date.

Price-Recognition Persistence asks whether the market continues repricing those economics. It remains separately dependent on surprise, prior recognition, valuation, Quality, financing/shareholder friction, materiality and later confirming or contradicting evidence.

Do not use economic persistence as a substitute for recognition analysis.

### Catalyst closeout and anti-double-counting

After realization:
1. close the old timer — do not keep an “imminent” bonus alive;
2. record the payoff-persistence state;
3. record any Activated Economic Stream;
4. update remaining opportunity / magnitude / recognition / capture / return velocity;
5. identify the next timer.

Do not count the same economics twice:
- contract signing can establish future committed economics;
- activation can reduce execution uncertainty and start revenue/cash conversion;
- do not add the full contract value again at activation;
- do not count already recognized revenue as new backlog;
- do not count a guidance increase caused by the same contract as an independent economic stream.

### Stack-to-Growth

For recurring/service businesses, overlapping activations can create:

```text
CONTRACTED STACK
→ DEPLOYMENT
→ ACTIVATED RUN-RATE
→ OVERLAPPING ACTIVATED RUN-RATES
→ STACK-TO-GROWTH
→ COMPOUNDING STACK if operating cash increasingly funds the next layer
```

This can strengthen remaining opportunity and company-level growth, but it does not bypass Quality or financing/shareholder-capture constraints.

## Rejected — CE/CQ as an independent sizing or allocation controller

Status: **REJECTED AS DIRECT CAPITAL AUTHORITY; RETAINED AS CANONICAL EVIDENCE STATE**.

Two preregistered/frozen tests now define the boundary.

### Mechanical portfolio translation — FAIL

An eight-case frozen A/B mapped CE rung / Commitment Quality directly to lower exposure. The treatment reduced loss severity in weaker contract cases, but materially undercaptured recognition-driven winners. Legacy contract treatment ended at approximately $1.167M versus approximately $1.073M for the mechanical CE/CQ sizing arm from the same $800k normalized starting capital.

Conclusion:

> **CE STATE / COMMITMENT QUALITY != P(MATERIAL PRICE MOVE | EVENT) != POSITION SIZE.**

### Fresh full-stack integration — NO-REGRESSION PASS / ZERO INCREMENTAL DELTA

A separate eight-case fresh cohort froze Company/OSE -> Quality -> TTC actions before outcome reveal. CE/CQ/decomposition was allowed to change evidence state but not bypass Quality/TTC.

Result:
- legacy and updated arms produced identical actions;
- normalized terminal capital was approximately $800.8k in both arms from $800k;
- naive buy-every-contract control ended near $773.2k under the same event-window convention;
- the updated module did not suppress the valid AVNW initiation or create a shadow allocation rule.

Interpretation:

> **Contract-state precision is useful evidence hygiene. Its capital value must flow through Quality, TTC profitability/recognition and downstream Quant. CE/CQ has not independently demonstrated alpha and may not size positions directly.**

## Rejected — FUNDED_ORDER-only autonomous capital gating

Status: **REJECTED AS A PROFITABILITY BRIDGE; ECONOMIC VEHICLE/FUNDED DISTINCTION RETAINED**.

A frozen 12-event bridge test compared the legacy autonomous SEC contract classifier with the patched fail-closed classifier. Manual contract-state rescue was disabled so extraction differences could propagate into a transparent diagnostic action rule:

```text
QUALITY PASS
AND autonomous intake contains FUNDED_ORDER
=> INITIATE

otherwise
=> CASH
```

The patched classifier was more economically precise about IDIQ / vehicle access, but the binary downstream implication `NOT FUNDED_ORDER => CASH` lost money versus legacy at every frozen horizon.

Pooled $1.2M normalized starting capital:

```text
30 sessions:
Legacy      $1,202,793  (+0.23%)
Patched     $1,194,141  (-0.49%)
Legacy edge            +0.72 pp

60 sessions — PRIMARY:
Legacy      $1,212,526  (+1.04%)
Patched     $1,196,516  (-0.29%)
Legacy edge            +1.33 pp

90 sessions:
Legacy      $1,243,789  (+3.65%)
Patched     $1,211,625  (+0.97%)
Legacy edge            +2.68 pp
```

The patched bridge avoided some losing vehicle trades, but it suppressed larger profitable recognition moves. A separate parser-recall miss on DCO also caused the patched arm to miss a real funded-order winner.

### Permanent interpretation

Reject:

> **If a contract event is not currently a FUNDED_ORDER, Xyool should automatically hold cash.**

Retain:

> **Vehicle / ceiling / optioned economics are not Committed Economic Value.**

And simultaneously retain the Recognition Firewall:

> **Economic commitment state does not determine short-term stock-recognition probability or capital action by itself.**

`CONTRACT_VEHICLE_ACCESS` must remain economically distinct from `FUNDED_ORDER`, but it must be allowed to enter TTC as a possible **recognition event** rather than being converted mechanically into CASH.

The bridge also reinforced the Quality prerequisite: the frozen Quality gate avoided large losses in AIRI, UAVS and KITT despite contract/order evidence.

## Current TTC freeze

At the allocation-rule improvement bottleneck, keep TTC structurally simple:

1. Company-level Xyool/OSE performs the Quality Qualifier and updates economic state.
2. Quality is an eligibility gate; a WATCH/FAIL name does not normally compete with qualified names merely because its catalyst is near.
3. TTC compares qualified opportunities and cash using causal distance-to-conversion, catalyst magnitude, probability, recognition, friction, and return velocity.
4. New capital goes to the best current qualified TTC opportunity.
5. Capital released by genuine quality/conversion/catalyst deterioration is rerouted through TTC.
6. Earned capital stays in an intact qualified thesis unless the higher removal/replacement hurdle is cleared.
7. Fresh material late-stage FES/current-holding catalysts raise the replacement hurdle.
8. Quant supports risk, concentration, ignition/recognition corroboration, and execution expression; Quant alone does not evict an intact qualified core.
9. Event-driven evidence updates are preferred to arbitrary calendar rebalancing.
10. Do not force reallocation among simultaneously strengthening qualified opportunities merely to maintain a ranking ladder.
11. Do not promote an opaque TTC master score until a frozen formula survives genuinely untouched mixed-universe validation.
12. When a material catalyst realizes, close its timer and record whether it ends economically or activates an ongoing stream; update remaining opportunity/recognition/return velocity without double-counting the original contract economics.

## Improvement bottleneck

The prior sequential protocol's stopping condition was met for additional generic allocation heuristics. The 2026-09-14 multicohort validation strengthens the architecture but does **not** reopen the door to arbitrary controller complexity.

The next improvement frontier is better **TTC information**, not more opaque allocation rules:
- better point-in-time catalyst-stage calibration;
- better probability and remaining-opportunity estimation;
- procurement-clock calibration;
- R&D conversion-clock calibration;
- catalyst-materiality measurement;
- shareholder-capture and dilution data;
- explicit catalyst closeout/reset states;
- payoff-persistence / activated-run-rate validation across business models;
- broader genuinely untouched historical universes;
- execution/cost realism;
- validated Quant overlays.

Do not add a new TTC heuristic merely because it explains one recent winner. It must survive point-in-time testing.
