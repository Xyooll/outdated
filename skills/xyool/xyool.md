# Xyool — Asymmetric Future-Multi-X Research Skill

## Purpose

Identify companies where **future economics are arriving faster than market recognition**, especially small-cap companies controlling bottlenecks or tollbooths.

Xyool is built from **ladders, trajectories, questions, evidence, and state transitions**, not a single composite equation. It separates:

1. **WHO?** — Can Xyool identify the company?
2. **WHEN?** — Can Xyool identify when repricing is becoming more likely?
3. **WHAT CHANGED?** — Can Xyool continuously update the model as new evidence arrives?

Never confuse a good business with a good stock setup. A company can have strong demand and still fail to create per-share value because of dilution, financing needs, execution, or valuation.

---

## Human Interface

Keep the visible analysis simple:

> **NEED → CONTROL → MOMENTUM → FRICTION → PRICE**

| Xyool lens | Question | State |
|---|---|---|
| NEED | What does the world increasingly need? | 🟢 🟡 🔴 |
| CONTROL | What is scarce, and who controls it? | 🟢 🟡 🔴 |
| MOMENTUM | Is the company's economic position strengthening? | 🟢 🟡 🔴 |
| FRICTION | What can prevent shareholders from capturing the value? | 🟢 🟡 🔴 |
| PRICE | Has the market recognized the destination? | 🟢 🟡 🔴 |

Complexity belongs underneath this interface.

# 1. THE RUNG ENGINE — BUILD + UPDATE

A ladder is **not** a static score. Every individual rung is a state with its own questions and evidence requirements.

For **every rung on every ladder**, maintain four question groups:

### A. Entry / Build Questions — “Why are we at this rung?”

Questions establish whether the company qualifies for the rung. They define the evidence needed to enter the state.

### B. Confirmation Questions — “Is this rung actually real?”

Questions test whether the evidence supporting the rung is independent, durable, measurable, and not merely narrative.

### C. Update / Transition Questions — “What changed?”

Questions are asked whenever new information arrives. They determine whether the company should:

- **↑ Advance** to the next rung
- **→ Maintain** the current rung
- **↓ Regress** to a lower rung

### D. Invalidation / Downgrade Questions — “What would break this rung?”

Explicitly identify evidence that would weaken or invalidate the state.

Each rung must therefore contain:

```text
RUNG
├── Entry / Build Questions
├── Confirmation Questions
├── Update / Transition Questions
├── Invalidation / Downgrade Questions
├── Required Evidence
├── Current State
├── Direction: ↑ / → / ↓
├── Confidence
└── Next Trigger
```

### Mandatory update rule

**Never simply re-score a company from scratch when new information arrives.** Compare the new information against the previous rung state.

```text
Previous State
    ↓
New Evidence
    ↓
What changed?
    ↓
Does it confirm, weaken, or invalidate the rung?
    ↓
Advance / Maintain / Regress
    ↓
Record why
```

### Rung transition principle

A rung is not advanced because one metric improved. Advance when the **evidence required by that rung** is satisfied. Regress when evidence supporting the current rung materially deteriorates.

This makes every ladder a **state machine**, not a static ranking.

# 2. IDENTIFICATION ENGINE — WHO?

Internal causal chain:

**DEMAND → DEMAND CASCADE → BOTTLENECK → POSITION → TRAJECTORY → CAPTURE → RESISTANCE → RECOGNITION**

The output is an **Xyool Candidate** when the evidence suggests a durable demand/capture/trajectory mismatch with current market recognition.

## NEED — Demand Ladder

Question:

> What will the world increasingly demand, and how durable is that demand?

### Demand Ladder

- **D0 — No structural demand**
- **D1 — Existing demand**
- **D2 — Growing demand**
- **D3 — Accelerating demand**
- **D4 — Strategic demand**
- **D5 — Structural demand**
- **D6 — Demand shock**

For every D rung, ask:

**Build:** What evidence establishes this demand state? Is demand real, measurable, and tied to actual customers/end users?

**Confirm:** Are orders, contracts, deliveries, utilization, industry data, or other independent evidence consistent with the state?

**Update:** Since the last assessment, did demand increase, decrease, accelerate, decelerate, broaden, narrow, become more strategic, become funded, or become less certain?

**Downgrade:** What evidence would move demand back to the prior rung?

Evaluate:
- demand size
- growth and acceleration
- duration
- strategic importance
- government/geopolitical support
- technology transition
- demographics/resources
- regulation/infrastructure
- funded demand
- contracts
- backlog
- delivered demand
- recurring demand

**Rule:** Do not substitute TAM for realized demand.

# 3. CONTROL — Bottlenecks and Tollbooths

Ask:

> Where does demand encounter scarcity, and who captures that scarcity?

## Demand Cascade

Map the chain:

**Primary demand → Secondary demand → Tertiary requirement → Infrastructure/input → Bottleneck → Supplier → Company**

Look beyond the obvious beneficiary. The best candidate may be one or more layers below the headline demand.

## Bottleneck Ladder

- **B0 — None**
- **B1 — Mild**
- **B2 — Specialized**
- **B3 — Qualification constrained**
- **B4 — Capacity constrained**
- **B5 — Sole-source / difficult to replace**
- **B6 — System bottleneck**

For every B rung, ask:

**Build:** What specifically makes this a bottleneck at this rung? Is scarcity observable in capacity, qualification, lead time, availability, switching cost, or system dependency?

**Confirm:** Is the constraint independently evidenced by customers, contracts, lead times, utilization, qualification rules, shortages, or supplier behavior?

**Update:** Has the bottleneck strengthened, weakened, migrated, been solved, or moved elsewhere in the chain? Did company capacity, competition, qualification, or substitution change?

**Downgrade:** What new supply, substitution, qualification, capacity, or technology would eliminate the bottleneck?

## Capture Ladder

- **C0 — Adjacent**
- **C1 — Participating supplier**
- **C2 — Qualified**
- **C3 — Preferred**
- **C4 — Critical node**
- **C5 — Sole-source / exclusive**
- **C6 — Systemically indispensable**

For every C rung, ask:

**Build:** What proves the company actually captures the bottleneck's economics?

**Confirm:** Is the relationship repeatable, qualified, contracted, difficult to replace, or economically critical?

**Update:** Did the company gain or lose customers, programs, qualifications, share, exclusivity, capacity, pricing power, or strategic importance?

**Downgrade:** What would cause customers to switch, qualify another supplier, insource, substitute, or reduce dependence?

Ask:

> If demand doubles, how much incremental economic value can this company realistically capture?

### Bottleneck Migration

Always ask:

> Where is the bottleneck moving next?

A bottleneck can migrate as capacity is added, technology changes, qualifications shift, or upstream shortages are solved. The next bottleneck may identify the next supplier before the market notices.

For every update, explicitly record:

**Old bottleneck → new constraint → company exposure → expected economic effect.**

# 4. MOMENTUM — Economic Trajectory

Question:

> Is the company converting its position into accelerating economics?

## Leading-to-Lagging Economic Chain

Xyool must distinguish **leading indicators** from **reported financial outcomes**.

```text
DEMAND
  ↓
ORDERS
  ↓
BOOKINGS / BOOK-TO-BILL
  ↓
BACKLOG
  ↓
NEW REVENUE STREAMS
  ↓
CAPACITY / DEPLOYMENT
  ↓
REVENUE
  ↓
GROSS MARGIN
  ↓
EBITDA / EARNINGS
  ↓
OPERATING CASH FLOW
  ↓
FREE CASH FLOW
```

### Backlog Questions

**Build:**
- Is there backlog?
- How large is it relative to revenue and capacity?
- Is it contracted?
- Is it funded, unfunded, cancellable, or conditional?
- What is the duration?
- Who are the customers?
- What is the composition?
- What percentage can convert within 12 months?

**Confirm:**
- Is backlog supported by signed contracts or credible purchase commitments?
- Is book-to-bill above 1?
- Are bookings and backlog independently consistent?
- Is backlog converting into revenue at the expected rate?

**Update:**
- Did backlog increase or decrease?
- By how much?
- Did new contracts get added?
- Did existing backlog convert into revenue?
- Did conversion accelerate or slow?
- Did funded backlog increase?
- Did cancellations occur?
- Did contract duration change?
- Did average contract size change?
- Did customer concentration change?
- Did capacity change?
- Did guidance change?
- Did the expected conversion timeline move?

**Downgrade:**
- Are cancellations rising?
- Is backlog aging?
- Is conversion slowing?
- Is backlog dependent on uncertain funding?
- Has the company lost capacity to fulfill it?

### New Revenue Stream Questions

A new income stream must be treated separately from ordinary growth.

**Build:**
- Is this genuinely new revenue or expansion of an existing stream?
- What created it?
- Is there a paying customer?
- Is revenue contracted?
- Is it recurring, transactional, or project-based?
- What market/customer class does it open?

**Confirm:**
- Has revenue actually started?
- Are multiple customers adopting it?
- Is there evidence of repeatability?
- Does it have credible pricing and margin economics?

**Update:**
- Did the stream launch?
- Did contracts increase?
- Did customer count increase?
- Did expected revenue contribution increase or decrease?
- Did margins improve or deteriorate?
- Did required capital change?
- Did the stream create a new bottleneck or strengthen an existing one?
- Did management move the expected contribution date?

**Downgrade:**
- Was launch delayed?
- Were contracts cancelled?
- Did customers fail to adopt?
- Did economics deteriorate?
- Did required capital become uneconomic?

### Economic Trajectory Ladder

- **T0 — Narrative only**
- **T1 — Revenue growth**
- **T2 — Margin improvement**
- **T3 — Earnings acceleration**
- **T4 — Operating leverage**
- **T5 — FCF compression**
- **T6 — FCF inversion**
- **T7 — FCF acceleration**
- **T8 — Self-funding growth**

For every T rung:

**Build:** What financial/operational evidence is required to enter the rung?

**Confirm:** Are multiple independent metrics moving consistently rather than one isolated metric?

**Update:** What changed since the prior assessment in orders, backlog, new revenue streams, deployment, revenue, margins, EBITDA, OCF, FCF, capacity, pricing, guidance, or working capital? Did the trajectory accelerate, remain stable, or weaken?

**Downgrade:** What deterioration would move the company back one or more rungs?

### Critical distinction

**Business trajectory ≠ per-share trajectory.**

Revenue can grow rapidly while dilution, converts, warrants, SBC, or financing requirements prevent comparable per-share value creation.

# 5. FRICTION — Shareholder Capture Resistance

Question:

> What can prevent shareholders from capturing the value being created?

Sources include:
- ATM issuance
- equity offerings
- dilution
- convertibles
- warrants
- debt
- interest expense
- capex
- cash burn
- stock-based compensation
- acquisition financing
- customer concentration
- production constraints
- regulatory friction
- technology substitution
- financing requirements
- execution risk

## Friction Ladder

- **F0 — Low**
- **F1 — Manageable**
- **F2 — Moderate**
- **F3 — Material**
- **F4 — Severe**
- **F5 — Equity-thesis threatening**

For every F rung, ask:

**Build:** What makes friction this severe? Quantify magnitude relative to cash, revenue, EBITDA, FCF, enterprise value, and share count where possible.

**Confirm:** Is the friction actually affecting shareholder capture, or is it merely potential?

**Update:** Since the last assessment, did dilution, debt, burn, capex, SBC, financing dependence, customer concentration, or execution risk increase, decrease, or change quality?

**Downgrade/Upgrade:** Did resistance compress enough to move lower, or increase enough to move higher?

Track three dimensions:

1. **Magnitude** — how large is the friction?
2. **Direction** — ↑ increasing / → stable / ↓ compressing
3. **Quality** — productive / neutral / destructive

High friction is not automatically bearish. Distinguish productive investment from financing used merely to sustain losses.

### Resistance Absorption

Ask:

> How much fundamental buying pressure is being absorbed by financial or structural selling pressure?

Compare price reactions to successive positive events. If strong earnings, contracts, or guidance produce increasingly larger price responses, absorption may be weakening.

### Resistance Compression

Watch for:
- dilution slowing
- debt declining
- cash burn falling
- capex beginning to produce revenue
- margins improving
- FCF approaching
- ATM dependence ending
- financing requirements declining
- share count stabilizing

A particularly attractive setup can be:

> **High Identification + High Resistance + Resistance Compressing + Low Recognition**

# 6. PRICE — Market Recognition Gap

The **Market Recognition Gap (MRG)** is the difference between fundamental progress and what the current price recognizes.

Conceptual form:

`MRG = Fundamental Progress − Price Recognition`

Relative expectation-gap form:

`MRG = (Evidence-Based Expected Fundamental Value − Implied Fundamental Value in Current Price) / Evidence-Based Expected Fundamental Value`

## Recognition Ladder

- **R0 — Ignored**
- **R1 — Narrative recognized**
- **R2 — Initial recognition**
- **R3 — Earnings recognized**
- **R4 — Estimate revisions**
- **R5 — Institutional recognition**
- **R6 — Valuation expansion**
- **R7 — Fully priced**

For every R rung, ask:

**Build:** What evidence shows the market has reached this level of recognition?

**Confirm:** Is recognition visible in price, volume, analyst estimates, institutional ownership, valuation, media attention, or investor behavior?

**Update:** Since the last assessment, did price, estimates, volume, institutional participation, valuation, or expectations change faster or slower than the underlying fundamentals?

**Downgrade:** Has recognition faded, expectations reset, or valuation compressed?

### Recognition Velocity

`RCR = −d(MRG)/dt`

Interpretation:
- large MRG + low recognition velocity = latent setup
- large MRG + accelerating recognition velocity = potential nonlinear phase

**Rule:** A stock having already risen does not prove the destination is priced. Compare current valuation with the future economic state implied by the trajectory.

# 7. CFIT — Cash-Flow Inversion Target

CFIT is the estimated point where the company transitions into **sustainable positive free cash flow**.

Sequence:

**BURN → STACK → PRE-INVERSION → OPERATING INVERSION → CASH-FLOW INVERSION → GROW**

Distinguish:
- destructive burn
- productive burn
- operating inversion
- cash-flow inversion

### CFIT Update Questions

**Build:** What assumptions establish the initial CFIT estimate? Include revenue growth, margin, capex, working capital, financing, and deployment assumptions.

**Confirm:** Are operating metrics moving consistently toward the assumptions?

**Update:**
- Did backlog increase?
- Did new revenue streams launch?
- Did deployment accelerate?
- Did revenue expectations change?
- Did margins change?
- Did capex change?
- Did burn compress?
- Did financing extend or shorten runway?
- Did expected FCF move closer to zero?

**Invalidate:** What evidence would push CFIT materially farther away?

Track:
- estimated CFIT period
- probability of inversion by date
- burn compression rate
- timeline velocity
- timeline acceleration
- inversion quality

Do not use CFIT to directly predict a stock price. Use it to identify when the financial trajectory may become sufficiently different to trigger recognition or repricing.

# 8. PARABOLIC TIMELINE

The timeline describes a **business/economic transition**, not a guaranteed stock-price path.

```text
NOW
 │
 ▼
STACK
 │  Backlog / contracts / capacity convert into revenue
 ▼
OPERATING INVERSION
 │  Revenue + margins + operating leverage accelerate
 ▼
CASH-FLOW INVERSION
 │  Sustainable FCF approaches/crosses zero
 ▼
RECOGNITION
 │  Estimates + institutions + valuation catch up
 ▼
PARABOLIC ZONE
    Price increasingly amplifies fundamental progress
```

## Timeline States

- **S0 — Narrative / Discovery**
- **S1 — STACK**
- **S2 — Operating Inversion**
- **S3 — Cash-Flow Inversion**
- **S4 — Recognition**
- **S5 — Parabolic Zone**
- **S6 — Mature / Reflexive**

For every S rung:

**Build:** What conditions establish the state?

**Confirm:** Are multiple causal indicators aligned?

**Update:** What changed in backlog, contracts, new revenue streams, deployment, margins, FCF, recognition, resistance, and catalysts?

**Downgrade:** Which missing or deteriorating condition breaks the state?

## Timeline Scenarios

Never give a parabolic date as certainty. Provide:

- **Bull timeline**
- **Base timeline**
- **Bear timeline**
- **P(Inversion by Date)**
- **P(Recognition Acceleration by Date)**
- **P(Parabolic Conditions by Date)**

### Timeline Velocity

Measure how quickly the transition estimate moves toward the present.

Example:

`Q4 2027 → Q2 2027` = strong positive velocity.

### Timeline Acceleration

Measure whether velocity itself is increasing.

Example:

`Q4 2027 → Q3 2027 → Q1 2027`

is stronger acceleration than:

`Q4 2027 → Q3 2027 → Q2 2027`.

Velocity becomes more meaningful when several independent operating indicators improve simultaneously.

# 9. IGNITION ENGINE — WHEN?

Question:

> When does the probability of a meaningful upward repricing materially increase?

Monitor:
- revenue acceleration
- gross-margin expansion
- EBITDA acceleration
- FCF improvement
- backlog acceleration
- book-to-bill
- guidance increases
- CFIT moving forward
- analyst estimate revisions
- revenue/EPS revisions
- guidance versus expectations
- price reaction to good news
- volume
- relative strength
- breakouts/pullbacks
- institutional participation
- catalyst proximity

## Ignition Ladder

- **I0 — Dormant:** fundamentals interesting, no price confirmation.
- **I1 — Pressure:** fundamentals improve while price lags.
- **I2 — Absorption:** positive information arrives but resistance absorbs much of the response.
- **I3 — Release:** resistance absorbs less and price response strengthens.
- **I4 — Ignition:** price consistently amplifies fundamental information.
- **I5 — Reflexive:** price rise → attention → capital/capacity → fundamentals → estimates → price.

For every I rung:

**Build:** What market/fundamental conditions establish this ignition state?

**Confirm:** Are price response, volume, relative strength, revisions, catalysts, and fundamentals consistent with the state?

**Update:** Since the last assessment, did price response strengthen or weaken relative to new information? Did estimates, volume, institutional participation, or catalyst proximity change?

**Downgrade:** Did the market stop responding, fundamentals weaken, resistance increase, or the catalyst fail?

### Key setup

> **High Identification + Fundamental Acceleration + Resistance Compressing + Low Recognition**

A second useful setup is:

> **High Identification + High Resistance + Resistance Compressing + Low Recognition**

Good news followed by little price movement can be more interesting than explosive price movement when the underlying thesis remains strong: fundamentals may be advancing faster than recognition.

# 10. EVENT-DRIVEN UPDATE PROTOCOL

Every new material event should trigger a **delta analysis**, not a full blind reset.

Material events include:

- earnings
- new contracts
- backlog changes
- new customers
- new revenue streams
- acquisitions
- divestitures
- capacity additions
- plant/data-center expansion
- qualification wins/losses
- pricing changes
- guidance changes
- financing
- debt repayment
- equity issuance
- convertibles/warrants
- regulatory changes
- major customer changes
- technology changes
- unexpected operational results

For each event ask:

1. **What changed?**
2. **Which rung(s) does it affect?**
3. **Is the change leading, lagging, or structural?**
4. **Does it confirm the current rung?**
5. **Does it justify advancing a rung?**
6. **Does it justify maintaining the rung?**
7. **Does it require regression?**
8. **Does it change the expected next rung?**
9. **Does it move CFIT?**
10. **Does it change resistance?**
11. **Does it change recognition?**
12. **Does it alter the bull/base/bear timeline?**
13. **What evidence must appear next to confirm the update?**

Record the delta as:

```text
Previous rung: T3
New evidence: backlog +42%, new contracts, utilization rising
Assessment: ↑
New rung: T4
Reason: operating leverage now supported by multiple leading indicators
Next confirmation: realized revenue + margin expansion
Confidence: 82%
```

# 11. RELATIONSHIP ENGINE — RUNG INTERACTIONS

A rung is more powerful when it causes another rung to change.

Xyool should map causal relationships such as:

```text
Demand ↑
   ↓
Orders ↑
   ↓
Backlog ↑
   ↓
Capacity constraint ↑
   ↓
Pricing / utilization ↑
   ↓
Revenue ↑
   ↓
Margin ↑
   ↓
FCF ↑
   ↓
CFIT moves forward
   ↓
Recognition probability ↑
```

But also:

```text
Demand ↑
   ↓
Capacity expansion required
   ↓
Capex / financing ↑
   ↓
Dilution / debt ↑
   ↓
Shareholder capture ↓
```

The system must therefore ask not only **“Did the rung improve?”** but:

> **“What other rung should move because this rung changed?”**

This is how Xyool turns isolated facts into a causal model.

# 13. RESEARCH DISCIPLINE

## Separate facts from inference

Label conclusions based on:
- reported facts
- management guidance
- contractual evidence
- third-party evidence
- calculated metrics
- Xyool inference
- probability estimate

Do not turn a narrative into a fact merely because it fits the framework.

## Bottleneck proof hierarchy

Prefer:

1. sole-source/exclusive evidence
2. qualification requirements
3. capacity constraints
4. long lead times
5. customer/program dependency
6. switching costs
7. specialized process/IP
8. generic supply/demand claims

## Trajectory proof hierarchy

Prefer:

1. realized revenue/orders
2. new contracted revenue streams
3. backlog conversion
4. backlog growth relative to revenue
5. margin expansion
6. EBITDA/earnings acceleration
7. operating cash-flow improvement
8. FCF improvement/inversion
9. guidance increases
10. estimates revising upward

## Financing proof hierarchy

Track actual filings and share counts. Do not infer dilution merely from the existence of an S-3, ATM, convertible, or warrant. Determine whether securities were issued, could be issued, on what terms, and whether the capital funds productive growth or persistent losses.

# 14. HISTORICAL / BLIND BACKTEST PROTOCOL

To test Xyool without hindsight:

1. Select historical companies without revealing winners or losers.
2. Freeze information at historical T0.
3. Assign the complete rung-level state:
   - Demand Ladder
   - Demand Cascade
   - Bottleneck Ladder
   - Position/Capture
   - Orders
   - Backlog
   - New Revenue Streams
   - Economic Trajectory
   - Resistance magnitude/direction/quality
   - Recognition
   - Ignition
   - CFIT/timeline state
4. Use only public information available before T0.
5. Reveal subsequent:
   - 20D return
   - 60D return
   - 120D return
   - 1Y return
   - maximum drawup/drawdown
   - next-quarter confirmation
6. Compare matched groups:
   - demand + bottleneck + low recognition
   - high demand without bottleneck
   - bottleneck without trajectory
   - trajectory without bottleneck
   - high identification + low ignition
   - high identification + ignition
   - high demand + high friction
   - high identification + resistance compressing + low recognition
   - backlog accelerating + low recognition
   - new revenue stream emerging + low recognition
   - backlog growth without conversion
   - revenue growth without per-share growth

### Core test

> Can Xyool identify the company before the nonlinear move, and can Ignition identify when the move may start?

A second test is:

> **Do rung transitions contain more predictive information than static ladder positions?**

A third:

> **Do leading indicators such as orders, backlog, and new revenue streams move the model earlier than reported revenue/FCF?**

Do not claim an exact-date backtest is complete unless the underlying historical data has actually been obtained and analyzed.

# 15. FAILURE MODES

Reject or downgrade candidates when:

- demand is only TAM/narrative
- bottleneck is asserted but not evidenced
- qualification/capture is unclear
- backlog is unlikely to convert
- backlog grows but conversion deteriorates
- new revenue streams are announced without commercialization evidence
- revenue growth does not reach margins/cash flow
- leading indicators improve but per-share economics deteriorate
- growth depends on repeated financing
- dilution outruns business growth
- debt/capex requirements overwhelm economics
- customer concentration creates unacceptable fragility
- valuation already assumes the destination
- management guidance is unsupported by operating evidence
- technology/commercialization remains too uncertain

The framework is explicitly designed to catch companies where **demand is right but equity economics are wrong**.

# 16. CORE PRINCIPLE

Xyool is not trying to find stocks that are merely cheap, popular, or growing.

It is trying to find situations where:

> **The future is arriving faster than the market is repricing it.**

The strongest candidate often has:

**Durable NEED + real CONTROL + accelerating MOMENTUM + compressing FRICTION + incomplete PRICE recognition**

But the deeper mechanism is:

> **Questions create and test the rungs. Evidence establishes the state. Update questions detect change. Rung transitions create trajectories. Relationships between trajectories create the thesis.**

The goal is not to predict a specific stock price. The goal is to identify the causal chain, locate the point where economics can accelerate, measure what can block shareholder capture, determine how each rung is changing, and identify when market recognition is beginning to catch up.
