# XYOOll / XYool — Catalyst Mapping & Iceberg Investment Skill

## Purpose

Identify speculative companies where the visible financial surface may understate the strategic value underneath it.

> **The surface is sometimes the tip of the iceberg.**

Do not assume a bad-looking company is secretly good. Prove whether its underlying relationships, assets, capital structure, and catalysts create a credible path from **STACK → GROW**.

## Workflow

### 1. Surface

Record what the market sees:
- Revenue and revenue growth
- Losses, cash and cash burn
- Market cap and share price
- Basic and diluted share count
- Debt and financing needs
- Obvious weaknesses
- Current market narrative

Do not stop here.

### 2. Relationship / Connection Mapping

Map the ecosystem:
- Customers
- Government agencies
- Defense primes
- Strategic partners
- Suppliers
- Acquisitions
- Investors and financing counterparties
- Geographic relationships
- Manufacturing relationships
- Distribution channels
- Technology/IP relationships
- Management/director connections

Rank connections by strategic importance and evidence quality.

### 3. Capital-Structure Reality Check

Separate:
- Existing shares
- Resale registrations
- Warrants
- Convertible securities
- Acquisition consideration
- Future shelf capacity
- Actual announced offerings
- Debt financing
- Potential dilution

**Rules:**
- A shelf registration is financing capacity, not an announced financing.
- A resale registration is not automatically new dilution.
- Acquisition stock issuance is not automatically bad dilution; evaluate what shareholders receive in exchange.
- Separate committed issuance from merely authorized future issuance.

### 4. Catalyst Mapping

Build a dated catalyst ladder. For each catalyst record:
- Event
- Deadline
- Current status
- Required conditions
- What it proves
- Probability
- Expected market impact
- B.S.G. classification

Prioritize hard deadlines, definitive agreements, closings, regulatory milestones, contract awards, production milestones, financing events, and revenue inflections.

Distinguish:
- Rumor → term sheet
- Term sheet → definitive agreement
- Definitive agreement → closing
- Relationship → contract
- Contract → revenue
- Revenue → scaling

### 5. B.S.G. Classification

#### BURN
Capital consumption used to build capability:
- R&D
- Capacity creation
- Acquisition spending
- Infrastructure investment

#### STACK
Strategic capability accumulation:
- Acquisitions
- Partnerships
- Technology/IP
- Customers
- Manufacturing capability
- Distribution
- Government/prime relationships
- Geographic expansion
- Strategic positioning

**STACK is not automatically growth.**

#### GROW
Measurable economic conversion:
- Signed contracts
- Purchase orders
- Revenue
- Production
- Recurring customers
- Backlog conversion
- Commercial deployment
- Government/prime awards
- Operating leverage

The critical transition is:

**STACK → GROW**

### 6. Iceberg Test

Ask:
1. Is the surface genuinely bad, or merely early?
2. What exists underneath the surface?
3. Which connections are independently verifiable?
4. What assets and relationships are being assembled?
5. What mechanism converts STACK into GROW?
6. Which dated events prove or disprove that mechanism?
7. What is the market currently pricing?
8. What could the company be worth if the hidden structure works?
9. How much capital/dilution is required to reach that outcome?
10. What evidence would invalidate the thesis?

### 7. Valuation Asymmetry

Model probability-weighted scenarios using:
- Current market cap
- Fully diluted share count
- Future capital requirements
- Potential dilution
- Revenue scenarios
- Strategic asset value
- Comparable valuations
- Catalyst-driven re-rating

Do not rely on a single price target. Give base, successful, strong, and exceptional cases with probabilities.

### 8. Options Overlay

Only after establishing the equity thesis:
- Match expiration to catalyst deadlines.
- Avoid expirations before important catalysts.
- Compare strikes by probability and payoff.
- Calculate breakeven and expiration value.
- Model intrinsic value at multiple stock prices.
- Consider IV, spread, theta, delta, and dilution.
- Treat cheap far-OTM calls as asymmetric probes, not evidence that the thesis is correct.

## Evidence Standard

Use primary filings and reliable independent sources when possible.

Never infer more than the evidence supports. Explicitly label:
- Confirmed
- Contracted but pending
- Management claim
- Probable inference
- Speculative scenario

## Output Format

For each company produce:

1. **Surface diagnosis**
2. **Hidden/underlying structure**
3. **Relationship map**
4. **Capital-structure map**
5. **Catalyst calendar**
6. **B.S.G. classification**
7. **STACK → GROW pathway**
8. **Bull/base/bear scenarios**
9. **Key invalidation conditions**
10. **Equity asymmetry**
11. **Options asymmetry**, when relevant
12. **Overall score**

## Core Investment Test

Do not hunt for hidden gems by intuition.

Look for:

**Surface weakness + strategic STACK + identifiable catalyst + credible GROW mechanism + low valuation = asymmetric opportunity.**

The objective is not to prove the company is good. The objective is to determine whether the market is pricing the visible surface while underpricing the iceberg.
